## LinkedIn

A $90,000 salary, two W-2 employees, and a 401(k) deferral capped at about $900. That is what happens to an owner when the plan fails nondiscrimination testing.

The scenario comes from our new post on an investor with 16 units across Visalia and Hanford and an S-corp management company. His leasing coordinator defers 1%, his maintenance tech defers nothing, so the ADP test limits him to roughly 1% of his own pay. Anything he already deferred gets refunded in the spring, and it is taxable.

A safe harbor design removes that dependency. For a calendar-year plan, the notice has to reach employees 30 to 90 days before the year starts, so December 1 is the practical deadline. A 3% nonelective contribution generally needs no annual notice. Match and QACA designs do.

One catch: rental income on Schedule E does not count as pay. You need W-2 wages or self-employment income to contribute, which means the S-corp salary and the plan belong in the same conversation.
