Most real estate investors we talk to in Visalia can recite their cap rates, but ask when the umbrella policy renews or which month the second LLC owes its annual report, and the answer is a pause. That pause is where money leaks. A year-end business checklist usually leans on tax moves, and those matter. The admin side of December is quieter and just as expensive when it gets missed: a lapsed policy, a property management agreement that renewed itself at the old rate, a state filing that went past due while nobody was looking.

December is the last low-stress window to fix all of it. Insurance agents have time to quote, vendors are open to a conversation, and nobody is yet buried in January 31 deadlines. Here is how to run the audit if you own rentals in Tulare, Kings, Fresno, or Kern County.

## Start with coverage while renewal quotes are still easy to compare

Insurance renewals tend to cluster in the last quarter and the first one. If you hold six or eight rentals, you may have a landlord policy per property, a commercial umbrella, and maybe an LLC-level liability policy, all renewing on different dates. Pull every declarations page and put the dates side by side.

Then check three things.

**Dwelling limits against rebuild cost.** A policy written in 2019 on a 1,400-square-foot Visalia rental may still carry the same dwelling limit. Construction costs have climbed since then, so the limit may now sit well under what it would take to rebuild. Ask your agent to rerun the replacement cost estimate, not just roll the old number forward.

**Rent loss coverage.** If a kitchen fire takes a unit offline for five months, the mortgage and property tax keep coming. Confirm the policy covers lost rent for the time a realistic repair would take, and ask what the waiting period is.

**Exclusions that matter locally.** Standard landlord policies typically exclude flood and earthquake. Whether to buy separate coverage depends on the property and your cash position, but it should be a decision you made on purpose rather than a gap you found out about later. Our post on [avoiding over-insuring in the wrong places](/resources/insuring-where-it-counts-avoid-over-insuring-in-the-wrong-pl) walks through how to size coverage against net worth and cash flow.

There is a tax angle too. Insurance premiums on rental property are generally deductible on Schedule E. Cash-basis taxpayers can often deduct a premium paid in December for a 12-month policy that runs into next year, as long as the coverage doesn't stretch past the end of the following tax year. Whether that works for your return depends on your accounting method and how the policy is written, so ask before you pay, not after.

## Read every lease and vendor agreement before it renews itself

Auto-renewal clauses are quiet. Nobody sends a reminder that your property manager's agreement rolled over for another 12 months at 8 percent of collected rent. On a portfolio collecting $12,000 a month, that is $960 a month, or $11,520 a year, and it is worth knowing whether the rate is still competitive.

Make a simple list of every recurring agreement and note the term, the renewal date, and the cancellation notice window:

- Property management agreements
- Landscaping, pest control, and HVAC maintenance contracts
- Your own office or storage lease, if you have one
- Software subscriptions for rent collection and bookkeeping
- Loan terms, especially adjustable-rate resets and balloon dates

The loan line is the one investors skip most often. A balloon payment due in 14 months is a refinancing conversation to start now, while rates, lender appetite, and your income documentation are all things you can still prepare. Waiting until six months out narrows your options.

On the tenant side, December is a good time to look at leases expiring in the first half of next year. Decide which units you want to renew, which should move to a new rate, and which are better off going month to month. California's statewide rent cap (AB 1482) and just-cause eviction rules cover many, though not all, rental units, so confirm with your attorney which of your properties fall under them before sending any rent-increase notice. Notice periods are strict, and a notice sent late can push a rent increase back by months.

For subscription-style costs, our guide to [stopping subscription creep](/resources/take-back-control-of-subscription-creep) shows how to audit recurring charges without spending a weekend on it.

## Put license, registration, and annual report dates on one calendar

This is where small lapses become real penalties. Investors who hold property through entities often have several overlapping obligations, and each one has its own date.

| Obligation | Who it affects | What to check in December |
|---|---|---|
| California LLC annual franchise tax | Every LLC doing business in California | The $800 minimum is due each year, and larger LLCs owe an additional fee based on gross receipts |
| Statement of Information (Secretary of State) | LLCs, filed every two years | Confirm your filing month, and know that a late filing can carry a $250 penalty |
| City business tax certificate | Investors operating in a city that requires one | Renewal month, which varies by city across Tulare, Kings, Fresno, and Kern counties |
| Out-of-state registration and annual report | Investors with properties in other states | Each state sets its own form, fee, and due date |
| Registered agent | Every entity | Is the agent still valid, and is the renewal paid? |

A concrete example: an investor with two LLCs, one holding four Visalia duplexes and one holding a Bakersfield fourplex, owes at least $1,600 in California minimum franchise tax every year before a single return is filed. Add a Texas rental held in a third entity and there is another registration, another report, and another date. This is the pattern we see with multi-state and multi-entity clients, and it is the reason we put every entity's filing dates on a shared calendar for each client.

The SBA has a useful overview of [licenses and permits](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/apply-licenses-permits) if you want to confirm which categories apply to your business. For local rental registration or business tax requirements, call the city clerk where each property sits. Requirements differ from one city to the next, and Visalia's are not Hanford's.

One tax note that surprises people: the fees themselves, such as franchise tax, registration fees, and license renewals, are generally deductible business expenses. Penalties and late charges are not. Filing on time saves you the penalty and keeps the deduction clean.

## Check the payment and paperwork dates that land right around the holidays

Two items tend to slip through the holiday weeks.

**California property tax.** The first installment is due November 1 and becomes delinquent after December 10. If you pay through an impound account, confirm the lender actually paid it. If you pay directly, a December 10 miss adds a penalty on every parcel, and on eight properties that adds up fast.

**Vendor paperwork for 1099s.** If you paid a contractor $600 or more during the year for repairs, turnover work, or maintenance, you generally need to report it, and the filing deadline is January 31. December is the time to collect missing W-9 forms, not the last week of January when the vendor is hard to reach. The [IRS small business page](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed) has the current forms and instructions.

If you also have W-2 employees or a bookkeeper on payroll, the deadlines are earlier still. See [why your payroll provider sets an early-December deadline](/resources/why-your-payroll-provider-sets-an-early-december-deadline-fo) for what to decide before the cutoff.

## A December timeline you can follow

You don't need to do this in one sitting. Spread across the month, it takes a few focused hours.

| When | Task |
|---|---|
| First week | Gather insurance declarations pages, lease and vendor agreements, and entity documents in one folder |
| By December 10 | Confirm first-installment property tax is paid on every parcel |
| Second week | Request renewal quotes, ask for updated rebuild estimates, and review rent loss and umbrella limits |
| Third week | Flag contracts with notice windows closing in Q1, and send cancellation or renegotiation notices where needed |
| Before the holidays | Request W-9s from any vendor missing one, and decide which year-end payments should be made before December 31 |
| Last week | Enter every license, annual report, franchise tax, and lease expiration date for the next 12 months into one shared calendar |

If your books are current, this audit is easy. If they are not, start with our [year-end bookkeeping checklist](/resources/close-the-books-before-the-year-closes-the-year-end-clean-up) first, because you can't see which expenses are recurring until the ledger is reconciled.

The audit and the tax plan feed each other. ## Common Questions

**When should I renew my business insurance in December?**

Start requesting quotes four to six weeks before the renewal date, which for many December policies means early to mid November. If you are past that, begin now. Agents can still quote quickly, and you'll have time to compare coverage limits, not just the premium price.

**Are business insurance premiums and license fees tax deductible for rental property owners?**

Generally yes. Premiums, license and registration fees, and California's LLC franchise tax are typically deductible against rental income. Penalties for late filings are not. Whether you can deduct a prepaid premium in the current year depends on your accounting method and the policy term.

**What is the annual state report deadline for a California LLC?**

California LLCs pay the $800 minimum franchise tax each year, and file a Statement of Information with the Secretary of State every two years. Your own due dates depend on when the LLC was formed or last filed, so check your account with the state, and confirm out-of-state entities separately.

**Should I review vendor contracts and leases every year?**

Yes, at least once a year, and December works well. Look for auto-renewal clauses, notice windows, and rate escalators. Catching a renewal 60 days early gives you room to renegotiate or switch, while catching it the week it renews usually does not.
